# SPEC: Newsroom

## Changelog

- **1.31 (2026-11-25)** — **Every Newsroom view links to where it is set up, and every setup view back to where it is read** (front end only). The owner could not find the watches: they sat on Following behind its "Alerts" switch, and the Alerts tab linked there only when a watch needed a look. Now: the Alerts tab always shows "Set up alerts →" (Following on Alerts, `/newsroom/following?kind=alerts`); the brief's masthead shows "Set up the brief →" (Following on "In your brief"); Following shows, beside its switch, "Read the brief →" or "See alerts →" and, on Alerts, a "New watch" button; the Follow button is on the Brief tab too and opens the form on "In your brief" (on Alerts it opens on alerts, on Following on the half shown). The empty brief's link goes to Following on "In your brief".
- **1.30 (2026-11-25)** — **The dashboard Brief card fits its sections** (ADR-0086, amended; front end only). The card has as many headline columns as it has sections, up to three (one column: four headlines, two: three each, three: two each), so a brief with two sections leaves no empty column. The edition's name is said once: by its tab when the card has tabs (the heading stays for screen readers, and the tab carries "New"), else as a plain heading; tabs, name, tags and the dateline are one row, with no heavy rule. A column's section label links to that section; the footer lists only the sections that have no column, beside "Read the whole brief", under one full-width rule. The top story's label is "Top story · <section>" on the same line as the column labels, and its outlets are named (three at most and "+N") in place of a count.
- **1.29 (2026-11-25)** — **Alerts that clear and say what they left out, and briefs that can be deleted** (ADR-0087). (1) A changed fact must cite where the earlier fact was written: `changes[].of` is 0 for the last known state (only when there was one) or the number of a recently reported title; a change that cites nothing or something not shown is dropped and the item stays as new (on vertex five of thirteen entries were marked Changed with "was: unknown"). (2) The prompt asks the headline and reason never to make an item sound more certain or more official than it is: a person's post, question, rumour, leak or joke is said to be one. (3) With `look_for` set, the items the model called not relevant (or gave no verdict) are kept in the journal marked `left_out`, with its reason as `why`, 7 days and at most 50 per watch (their own bound, so they never push a finding out); they are not in the feed, its unread count or the brief's watches block. `GET .../feed?left_out=true` lists them; `POST .../feed/keep` (`{ids}`) puts them in the feed, read. (4) `GET .../feed/unread` answers `{count, left_out}`: unread feed entries and kept left-out items; the Alerts tab shows it and the sidebar's Newsroom dot names it beside unread briefs (one shared 30 s poller). Opening an alert's details marks it read, as opening its link does. The Alerts tab shows "Alerts | Left out by the model · N" when a watch has `look_for` (`left_out` in the same answer counts the kept items), and a warning line naming each switched-on watch that needs a look (source failing, sources not answering, send failed, no channel, could not compare), with a link to Following; nothing when all is well. The alert row is unchanged. (6) The brief no longer says "It was written automatically at …, nothing to press, the next one is …" under the dateline: the dateline says when it was written and the end mark when the next comes; the line is shown only when the brief is not today's. (5) `DELETE .../briefs/issues/{id}` deletes one written brief and `DELETE .../briefs/issues/{id}/older` the briefs of the same edition made before it (the named one stays), each answering `{deleted}`; the already-shown record is kept, and the slot of a deleted brief is remembered (`deleted` in `briefs.json`, kept 30 days) so the scheduler does not write it again. The brief's masthead has a trash button with the two choices, each behind a confirmation.
- **1.28 (2026-11-25)** — **One way to follow, story feedback and OPML** (ADR-0086; front end only, no route or stored shape changes). (1) **Follow**: the Newsroom's one way to follow something is the Follow form on Following (header button "Follow"; open while the address carries `?follow=`): the words to follow, then In my brief (a topic whose search is the words, in the section picked; it joins every brief that already reads that section), Alert me at once (a watch with a news search for the words, hourly, with optional "only items about" and a connector) or Both; an optional feed is read as well, and Preview shows what a news search finds now through the source look-up. The full watch form is one link away ("every option"). (2) Each story in the brief offers **More like this** (the words the owner picks from its headline become one more search of the story's topic, at most 10), **Less like this** (the picked words become the topic's skip words, at most 20: a story whose headline or text holds one is not collected) and **Follow this story** (the Follow form, as an alert, with its first words). The story's topic is the first topic of its section. The prompts are unchanged. (3) **My sources** imports an OPML file (each feed address once, http or https only, read as data; a repeat or a full list is refused per feed and counted) and exports the owner's own feeds as OPML.
- **1.27 (2026-11-25)** — **The Newsroom is a reader, cards link into it, and the fixes of the review** (ADR-0086; plan `newsroom-review-2026-11-25.md`). (1) The page opens on the brief; tabs are Brief, Alerts (the feed), Following (the brief's topics and editions, and the watches) and Settings; `/newsroom/briefs` and `/newsroom/watches` redirect, and the phone summary links to `/newsroom`. The brief is a nameplate with one dateline, the lead and top stories, every section open (no folding), a section index (a sticky column on a wide screen, a row on a phone), a side column in reading order (critical facts said once, chips, your day, alerts, the rest of Home, Model use, which no longer needs the Home switch), a tick per outlet on each story, an end mark, Print and Copy as text (no home fact, reminder or watch in the text), and j / k / o to move and open. (2) A link names its place: `/newsroom?edition=&section=&story=`; sections and stories are anchors (`brief-section-<topic id>`, `brief-story-<pool id>`). The Brief card is a front page (what needs a look, the lead, two headlines from up to three sections, the sections as links; no tabs); every card link carries the edition. (3) **Write by hand is a job**: `POST .../editions/{id}/issue` answers `202` with `{edition_id, state: writing, step, started_at}` at once and the write runs in the stack's own goroutine (stopped with the stack); `GET .../briefs/jobs` lists each edition's write (`step: {stage: reading|writing, done, total, section}`, then `done` with `issue_id` or `failed` with `problem`, kept 10 minutes); a second write of one edition while one runs is refused (unavailable). (4) The watches and the briefs each have their own loop, so writing an edition never holds up a watch. (5) `GET .../briefs/issues` lists `{id, edition_id, made_at, read}` only; the page reads the one issue it shows by id. (6) The pool is its own file, `briefs-pool.json` (a manifest Storage entry); a change that is not a collection rewrites only `briefs.json`. The pool kept inline in an older `briefs.json` is not read; with no pool file beside `briefs.json` every topic is marked never read, so the next pass collects them all and no edition is written from an empty pool. (7) Per edition, set under Fine-tune: `stories_per_section` (1 to 10, default 5), `top_stories` (1 to 8, default 4), `repeat_after` (1 h to 7 days, default 24 h) and `max_story_age` (6 h to the pool's 72 h, default 48 h); a zero reads as the default, and the shown record is kept 7 days. (8) The core bounds each Fetch port request at 30 s from first byte sent to last byte read (`stackport.fetchTimeout`), so a source that trickles its body cannot hold a stack's loop. (9) Colour classes name only tokens that exist (`tokenClasses.test.ts`); the warning text uses `state-running`.
- **1.26 (2026-11-25)** — **The leftovers are built** (ADR-0083, 0084, 0085, amended). (1) The critical set is now `radio_open_or_weak_ap` and `radio_rogue_twin` (the owner approved it). The open Model use block draws them as 24 columns, labelled 00, 06, 12, 18. (5) A chip row, live and frontend only (it is not in the written edition): automation runs that failed in the last day (link to Automations) and alerts no connector took (link to the connector settings); a chip shows only above zero and a source that cannot be read shows nothing. Not built, by the owner's choice: cost for cloud models, and a message on a quiet day.

- **1.10 (2026-11-22)** — **The edition is written.** The Briefs module composes each edition once per ready time, starting 20 minutes ahead (catching up for up to 3 hours after a stop): per section it caps the candidates (15, best by outlets, none older than 48 h), asks the model to choose up to 5 and word them, keeps only entries that cite a shown story, and makes one more call for the top 4. - **1.0 (2026-11-20)** — First version. The Newsroom is a stack (`stacks/newsroom`, module `topics`) that follows topics, reads their sources on a timer, keeps what is new in a feed and, for a topic with a channel, hands the core a notice. The Morning Paper module, the email connector and Reddit and Bluesky sources are not built (plans: `autonomy/morning-paper.md`, `cross-cutting/email-connector.md`).

## Contract

**Watches.** A watch has a name, one to ten sources, an interval (default 1 h, floor 5 min, sent as a Go duration string), a news window `max_age` (default 72 h; older posts are skipped, see 1.6), an on/off flag and an optional channel (a connector type; empty means the feed only). It may say what to look for: `look_for` (the owner's words, at most 300 characters), `keywords` (an item must contain one) and `skip` (an item containing one is dropped), each list at most ten words of 40 characters, lower-cased and de-duplicated. A watch made by the assistant is on and feed-only and takes the same fields. Stored in one file, `watches.json`, in the stack's data folder; a factory reset empties it.

**Sources** (guarded `Fetch` only: GET, no header, 1 MiB): RSS or Atom (also GitHub releases), Hacker News search, Mastodon tag, a web page (compared by its visible text), a public Telegram channel preview (per-channel opt-in, at most one read per 10 min), a **news search** (`newssearch`: Google News RSS search, keyless; the target is the query and the instance a region such as `en-GB`, default `en-US`; every item is headline only, with its publisher moved out of the title and no text, and its link is a Google page, not the article). Each kind's target is checked when a watch is saved (`sources/validate.go`, mirrored in the form by `utils/newsroom/sourceCheck.ts`): an https address with a host for a feed or page, 1 to 200 characters for a search, a 4 to 32 character channel name, a bare tag and a plain host for Mastodon. Pasted forms are reduced to the bare value first: `https://t.me/s/aipost` or `@aipost` gives `aipost`, `#golang` gives `golang`, `https://Mastodon.social/` gives `mastodon.social`. `Fetch` itself stays strict. A source that cannot be read is `failing` when none can be, and is kept in the watch's `last_problems` when others answered; it is never silently "nothing new".

**A run** (one watch at a time; the timer loop ticks every minute, a manual run is refused while the module is off): read every source; items already in the seen ledger (60 days, 2000 per watch) are dropped; keywords and skip words drop more (recorded as seen, no model); the first successful read of a watch is a **baseline**: nothing is announced, but up to five of the items that match are put in the feed marked "First check" (with `look_for` set and no model, none); for new items the model step (the `model` port, ADR-0071) says which are new, changed (a was and now pair citing the last known state or a shown recent title, 1.29) or repeats of a shown recent title, and writes a short state note. When `look_for` is set the same call gives a verdict on every item (`why`, then `relevant` true or false); an item it called not relevant is recorded as seen, named in the row ("N did not match what you look for") and kept aside in the journal as `left_out` with its reason (1.29). An item with no verdict is not shown (named in the row); an answer with no "verdicts" list, no model, or a spent daily limit when `look_for` is set is `analysis_failed`: nothing is shown or recorded and the run retries. The reason text is never read to decide. Code checks the answer: a repeat or verdict must cite a shown item or title. With no model chosen, or the daily limit (200 calls) spent, items go to the feed as new with the row "not compared". Any other model failure is `analysis_failed`: nothing is recorded and the run retries.

**Delivery.** The core words and sends the notice through the `notice` port (ADR-0072); the stack never sends. A refused channel shows `no_channel`; a failed send records nothing and retries. Every change is kept in the feed whether or not a message is sent.

**Feed.** Entries are kept 30 days, 200 per watch; left-out items 7 days, 50 per watch, and only `?left_out=true` shows them. The server groups entries by day then watch (newest entry `current`, the rest `earlier`); the screen reads a day as one list, newest first, and names the watch on a row only when more than one watch has entries. Repeats fold under the entry they repeat ("also seen"). Days are drawn in the zone the screen sends (`?zone=`), else UTC.

**Routes** (admin): `GET|POST /admin/api/newsroom/watches`, `PUT|DELETE .../watches/{id}`, `POST .../watches/{id}/run`, `GET .../feed` (`watch`, `unread`, `zone`), `POST .../feed/read`, `GET .../feed/unread` (`{count, left_out}`), `POST .../feed/keep` (`{ids}`: left-out items into the feed, read), `POST .../feed/clear` (`{ids}` | `{scope:"read"|"all"}`; deletes entries from storage for good; the seen ledger is kept, so a cleared item does not return from the same source), `GET .../sources/lookup` (`kind`, `target`, `instance`: what one source would read, as `{found, target, instance, title, sample[≤3], choices[], problem}`; nothing is saved; the value is checked first and a bad one is a problem with no request; a problem about the moment such as internet off is not remembered) and `GET .../sources/suggest` (`kind`, `instance`, `target`: Mastodon only, trending tags or tags matching what is typed; other kinds answer an empty list). Both are used by the watch form and the brief topic form, so they refuse only when Watches **and** Briefs are both switched off (1.12), and read only through the guarded `Fetch`. **Tools** (attended runs, `general` profile only, hidden and refused while the module is off): `newsroom_follow`, `newsroom_watches`.

**Module.** `newsroom.disabled` is the switch. Starting needs internet access allowed in Guardrails. No timer runs while the module is off.

**Screens** (SPEC-003): the Newsroom page, tabs Brief (the page without a tab), Alerts, Following and Settings (the module switches), at `/newsroom/:tab?` (1.27); the watch form offers suggestions for what to look for, a guide per kind of source, and under each source a **Look up** that shows what it would read (name, latest items, the cleaned-up value) and offers feeds a page advertises or a Mastodon server's tags (a hint: saving does not need it); a dashboard card `newsroom-latest` that shows the latest headlines and a failing flag and links only (ADR-0064). All text is escaped; a link opens only if it is http or https.

**Briefs** (module `briefs`, ADR-0075; the news sections of an edition are built, see 1.10; the screens, watches, Home, Your day and Telegram are not): a **topic** is a name, optional category, news-search queries with a region, feeds and other readers (feed, news search, Hacker News, Mastodon; never Telegram or a page), ignore words; an **edition** is a name, a ready-by time and weekdays (read in the stack's timezone through the schedule port), how often and for how long before it collects (default every 2 h for 12 h; 1 h to 12 h, and 1 h to 24 h), and up to 12 sections, each a news section of one topic (none when it has watches or home). The loop collects a topic when some enabled edition that lists it is inside its window and the topic was last collected at least that edition's interval ago (the shortest wins); **collecting never calls a model**. Collected items go into one **pool**: a story is one item with a report per outlet, found by normalised title or by link, kept three days, at most 3000. A source that cannot be read is named on its topic (`last_problems`) and cleared when it reads again. Routes (admin only, all follow the module switch where they read the internet): `GET|POST .../briefs/topics`, `PUT|DELETE .../topics/{id}`, `POST .../topics/{id}/collect` (and `POST .../editions/{id}/issue` first collects the edition's topics never read or older than its interval, 1.12), `GET|POST .../briefs/editions`, `PUT|DELETE .../editions/{id}`, `GET .../briefs/pool?topic=`, (1.12) `GET|POST .../briefs/sources` and `DELETE .../sources/{id}` (the built-in catalog then the owner's own list, `my_sources` in `briefs.json`, at most 100; each entry is `{id?, label, category, kind: feed|newssearch, target, builtin}`; a repeat of an entry in either list is refused; a topic copies the pick and keeps its `label`), and (1.10) `POST .../editions/{id}/issue`, `GET .../briefs/issues`, `GET .../issues/{id}`. Durations in these routes are strings such as `2h`. The stack's model calls (watches today, editions later) take turns through one gate and are never refused for another caller's call.

**Briefs since 1.27.** Files: `briefs.json` (topics, editions, written editions, the shown record, my sources) and `briefs-pool.json` (the pool); a collection writes both, pool first, anything else only the first; with `briefs.json` and no pool file every topic is due. An edition also carries `stories_per_section` (1–10, default 5), `top_stories` (1–8, default 4), `repeat_after` (1 h–7 days, default 24 h: a shown story stays out unless more outlets report it) and `max_story_age` (6–72 h, default 48 h); the routes send the two spans as strings and always return all four with the defaults filled in. `GET .../briefs/issues` lists `{id, edition_id, made_at, read}` (newest first, at most 30); `GET .../issues/{id}` is the whole issue. `POST .../editions/{id}/issue` answers `202` with the write `{edition_id, state, step, started_at}` and runs it in the background (reading the stale topics, then each section, then the top stories); `GET .../briefs/jobs` lists the writes (`writing`, then `done` with `issue_id` or `failed` with `problem`, kept 10 minutes); a second write of one edition while one runs is refused. `DELETE .../issues/{id}` and `DELETE .../issues/{id}/older` delete written briefs (1.29). The watches and the briefs run in two loops. Each Fetch port request ends after 30 s.
